# Frozen seam-regularized placement: negative ablation

We tested whether a simple spatial-smoothness prior improves the **early-exit router map** without changing its depth histogram or analytical synthesis MAC. Before opening any source image for quality, [`make_kodak_seam_plan.py`](../../../proof/cpu_early_exit/make_kodak_seam_plan.py) froze all 120 Kodak image-QP maps and was committed. For each map it enumerated every unique permutation of the router's six selected exits and maximized the sum of decoder-visible cost-adjusted router scores minus `0.5 ×` the sum of absolute exit-index differences across the seven four-neighbour tile edges. The 0.5 score-space penalty was chosen from score-margin and map-change diagnostics on Kodak, **not** from reconstruction quality. It changes 14/120 maps.

Actual replay shows **no improvement**: the mean RGB PSNR change is **−0.00021 dB**, with a paired 24-image cluster-bootstrap 95% interval **[−0.00055, +0.00006] dB**. Of 14 changed maps, six improve and eight worsen; the changed-only mean is −0.00181 dB and the worst loss is 0.01502 dB. All other 106 maps are exactly unchanged. At QP63, all four changed maps worsen, giving a 24-image mean of −0.00134 dB [−0.00292, −0.00016]. The selected maps retain exactly the original exit counts and modelled MAC; all changed-case baseline MSEs reproduce the previous bitstream replay to 1e-12.

This is a mechanism diagnostic, not a general theorem that spatial regularization never works. A fixed uniform total-variation penalty ignores that an apparently sharp boundary can be the router's correct way to spend extra depth on a difficult tile. The QP63 failures are consistent with the separate finding that placement value increases at high QP, but they do not prove that mechanism. Kodak has informed prior project work and the penalty was chosen after inspecting its **decoder-visible scores**, so the experiment is exploratory rather than an independent holdout. No quality-based tuning, encoder-side information, rate reduction or latency measurement is implied.
