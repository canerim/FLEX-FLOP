\begin{abstract}
Spatially adaptive decoding can save computation by stopping easy regions
early, but a large total saving need not imply a large benefit from content
adaptation. We study this distinction in the intra decoder accompanying
DCVC-UF. A shared synthesis trunk feeds tile-level exits, pointwise adapters
and a full-frame reconstruction head, while the analysis transform and entropy
model remain fixed. We compare source-informed exit search and a learned
router with uniform depth and ordered dithering on the same recorded mixed
reconstructions. Across 53 first intra frames and five quality indices, the
router saves \MainRouterSaving\% of modelled decoder multiply--accumulates at
a 0.1\,dB target, compared with \MainDitherSaving\% for dithering on 263 common
frame--quality pairs. The paired advantage is \MainPremium\ percentage points
and falls to \LoosePremium\ points at a 0.3\,dB target as the exit ladder
saturates. Evaluation of the final cropped images also reveals target-budget
violations that tile-level calibration does not eliminate. Historical timing
isolates a further cost: acquiring source-error tables at the encoder.
These findings identify where spatial routing adds value and which quality,
signalling and execution costs must be included before arithmetic savings can
be interpreted as practical acceleration.
\end{abstract}
