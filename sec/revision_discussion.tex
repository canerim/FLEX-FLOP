\section{Discussion}

FLEX-UF exposes two operating regimes. Tight quality constraints leave room
for useful learned placement. Near the shallowest-exit ceiling, blind
mixtures approach the same arithmetic cost, and routing overhead can outweigh
the remaining margin. A practical stopping policy should therefore be chosen
jointly with the quality budget and measured device cost.

The principal limitation is the gap between this allocation evidence and a
complete codec benchmark. The current controls use source calibration,
reported losses use the fine-tuned full-depth anchor, and MAC counts exclude
routing and signalling. A common-reference quality replay and a held-out
control sweep are required before a practical guarantee can be claimed.
Actual bytes and paired wall-clock timings must then include entropy work,
packing, tile movement and fallbacks. Perceptual metrics and boundary crops
also remain necessary; a small RGB PSNR loss alone does not prove visual
indistinguishability.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented FLEX-UF, a DCVC-UF spatial early-exit design that combines a
shared synthesis stem, conditional tile depth, pointwise adaptation and
full-frame repair. Recorded reconstructions show substantial decoder
arithmetic reductions and a further, budget-dependent gain from learned
placement over dithering. A common delivered-loss analysis preserves that
trend. Controlled depth and complete-bitstream experiments are the next
tests of whether these arithmetic gains produce a practical codec speedup.
