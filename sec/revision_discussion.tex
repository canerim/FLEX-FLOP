\section{Discussion}

FLEX-UF exposes two operating regimes. Tight quality constraints leave room
for useful learned placement. Near the shallowest-exit ceiling, blind
mixtures approach the same arithmetic cost, and routing overhead can outweigh
the remaining margin. The design implication is to allocate computation
jointly over content and budget: a sophisticated selector is useful only
where its decisions change the attainable quality--cost trade-off.

This principle connects early exit to the independent depth bank without
requiring a cascade of both mechanisms. Shared exits reuse features and
retain one payload; independent experts can specialise more freely but
introduce separate representations, model residency and expert batching.
The controlled study is intended to establish when those additional degrees
of freedom justify their systems costs. Combining a bank with multiple
exits per expert would add another axis and is outside the current design.

The principal empirical limitation is the gap between allocation evidence
and a complete codec benchmark. Current controls use source calibration,
reported mixed losses use the fine-tuned full-depth anchor, and synthesis
MAC counts exclude entropy recovery, routing and signalling. A sequence-disjoint control diagnostic in
the supplement exposes the difference between mean loss and tail risk;
common-reference final-image replay is still required before a practical
guarantee can be claimed.
Actual bytes and paired wall-clock timings must include entropy work,
packing, tile movement and fallbacks. Boundary crops and perceptual metrics
are also needed; RGB PSNR alone does not establish visual equivalence.

\section{Conclusion}
FLEX-UF makes DCVC-UF synthesis a spatial allocation of computation.
Its evaluated early-exit path shares a latent and prefix, lets tiles stop at
different depths, and restores a complete image through aligned exits and
full-frame repair. Recorded reconstructions show substantial synthesis
arithmetic savings, with a further, budget-dependent benefit from learned
placement over dithering. The independent six-depth study extends the same
allocation question to model selection. Matched rate--quality and complete
execution measurements will determine how the benefits of reuse and
specialisation translate into practical codec efficiency.
