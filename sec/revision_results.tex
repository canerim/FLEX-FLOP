\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
We evaluate the first intra frame of 53 video sequences at QP values
$\{0,16,32,48,63\}$, giving 265 frame--QP pairs. Every policy uses the same
pinned e15 shared-latent checkpoint, the same tile geometry and the same
per-exit cost vector. The learned rule uses one fixed router checkpoint.
Six target budgets span 0.05--0.5\,dB. The source-calibrated archive contains actual mixed reconstructions.
Additional CPU experiments test fixed controls, the released reference and
component dependence. Every numerical decoder comparison concerns DCVC-UF;
no experiment here measures complete GPU codec latency.

Two complementary protocols answer different questions. The \emph{nominal
sweep} compares maps selected under the same table target, retaining the
common feasible cases of all four rules: 204 pairs at 0.05\,dB, 263 at
0.1\,dB and 265 at each larger target. The \emph{delivered-cap analysis}
reselects from the outputs already measured at those targets under a common
cropped YCbCr~4:4:4-loss cap, with the full-frame e15 output as an explicit fallback.
It retains all 265 pairs at every cap. The second protocol is retrospective
and source-aware; its cost omits acquiring and rejecting candidates.

Confidence intervals use 5,000 paired bootstrap draws over sequences,
keeping QPs from the same sequence together. They quantify sequence-sample variation conditional on fixed weights and
controls, without including training or calibration uncertainty. The
supplement also reports a conservative content-group sensitivity check. A separate
fixed-cohort comparison retains the same 204 pairs across the entire nominal
sweep, avoiding changes of sample membership as the target relaxes.

\subsection{The reconstruction value of depth}

The stored uniform-exit reconstructions provide a direct depth control.
Across all 265 pairs, their mean padded YCbCr~4:4:4 losses against the released
full-frame reference are \UniformLossSix/\UniformLossEight/\UniformLossTen/\UniformLossTwelve\,dB at 6/8/10/12 blocks.
Their modelled MAC savings are 39.12/24.22/13.03/$-0.95$\%, respectively.
Additional depth has diminishing average benefit and non-uniform local
benefit: mean within-frame tile-gain IQRs are 0.075/0.034/0.024\,dB for
successive block pairs. The supplement gives the distributions and paired
intervals. These measurements concern actual uniform maps on padded YCbCr~4:4:4;
they motivate spatial allocation but are not cropped mixed-map losses or
results for independently trained shallow codecs.

\subsection{Early exit reduces work; routing improves its allocation}

At the nominal 0.1\,dB target, FLEX-UF with learned routing saves
\MainRouterSaving\% of modelled synthesis MACs. Source-informed search reaches
\MainOracleSaving\%, while uniform depth and dithering reach
\MainUniformSaving\% and \MainDitherSaving\%, respectively
(\cref{fig:budget}). The ordering separates three effects. Shallower
synthesis supplies most of the reduction. Mixing adjacent depths improves
the available cost resolution. Content-dependent placement contributes a
further \MainPremium\ percentage points over dithering, with a paired 95\%
interval of [\MainPremiumLow,\MainPremiumHigh].

\subsection{The gain persists under a common delivered-loss cap}

Does the margin persist when every selected output meets the same measured
loss cap? For each policy and frame--QP pair, we deduplicate the maps
measured at the six nominal targets, retain those meeting the cropped
$\Delta_{444}$ cap within $10^{-4}$\,dB, and choose the cheapest. Repeated
maps have identical recorded losses and costs. The evaluated e15 full-frame
output supplies a zero-loss, zero-saving fallback; no quality point is
interpolated.

\Cref{tab:cap} reports the 0.1\,dB case. The router saves \CapRouterSaving\% versus
\CapDitherSaving\% for dithering, a \CapMainGain-point paired difference [\CapMainLow,\CapMainHigh]. Their
mean achieved losses are also close: \CapRouterLoss{} and \CapDitherLoss\,dB. Only one router
case uses the full-frame fallback, compared with two dither cases.
The margin survives a common measured cap. Selection remains retrospective
and source-aware, omits candidate-acquisition cost, and searches only the
recorded outputs.

\subsection{Why the routing margin contracts}

The cheapest reachable depth imposes a \MacCeiling\% ceiling on the
arithmetic model. As the target relaxes, more tiles can take that exit and
there are fewer consequential spatial decisions. The nominal router--dither
margin declines from \MainPremium\ points at 0.1\,dB to
\MediumPremium\ at 0.2\,dB, \LoosePremium\ at 0.3\,dB and
\SaturatedPremium\ at 0.5\,dB. Under the delivered-cap protocol the margin
similarly falls from 2.93 to 0.35 points between 0.1 and 0.3\,dB. The
fixed-cohort result and QP breakdown in the supplement indicate that this
behaviour is not explained solely by a changing feasible subset.
At the 0.1\,dB delivered cap, routing saves more in 170 pairs, less in 24,
and ties in 71. At 0.5\,dB, 262 of 265 pairs tie; the residual gain comes
from one sequence.

The maps in \cref{fig:maps} make the mechanism visible. At the 0.1\,dB
target, the router spends different depths on different regions. At
0.3\,dB, search, routing and dithering all assign depth six on this frame,
saving 39.1\% MACs at 0.201\,dB measured YCbCr~4:4:4 loss. We select this example from the five archived thumbnails by proximity to
the aggregate 0.1\,dB margin. Source luma supplies context, not a visual
quality comparison.

A histogram-preserving diagnostic further separates spatial placement from
exit proportions. Randomly permuting a map leaves its cost unchanged. We
compute the exact expected table MSE under that permutation and compare it
with the recorded assignment. At 0.1\,dB, the router's mean placement gain
is 0.0213\,dB [0.0167,0.0262], whereas dithering's is 0.0007\,dB
[$-0.0002$,0.0017]. This table-based association holds the histogram fixed; replaying permuted
maps through the final head remains a separate ablation.

\subsection{Fixed controls test the routing advantage}
\label{sec:fixedcontrol}

Source-calibrated allocation gives the selector information that a cheap
stopping rule would ideally avoid. We therefore fit its scalar control on
four sequence folds and apply it to the fifth, keeping all QPs of each
sequence together. Calibration minimises compute subject to a mean or
90th-percentile (Q90) table-loss target. We then replay every fixed map
at QP32 through the actual network: 53 frames, three policies and two
controls give 318 reconstructions, with no quality-based fallback.

\Cref{tab:fixedcontrol} reports cropped RGB loss against e15 full-frame
synthesis. Q90 calibration lowers the observed frequency of losses above
0.1\,dB, at the cost of less arithmetic saving. The router--dither saving
difference is 1.87 points under mean control, with a paired 95\% interval
of $[-1.60,5.19]$, and 2.54 points under Q90, with interval
$[-0.42,5.54]$. Both include zero, and the policies do not have exactly
matched achieved quality. Thus the source-calibrated margin has not yet
been established for fixed controls.

\subsection{Checking the mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanismchecks}

\paragraph{Exit alignment and repair.}
With the checkpoint and 53 Q90 maps fixed, we replace adapters, repair,
or both by identity and retain all images. Adapter removal costs
1.2309\,dB mean RGB PSNR, with a paired 95\% interval of
$[0.8765,1.6582]$. Repair removal costs only 0.00208\,dB
$[0.00060,0.00363]$; removing both costs 1.2538\,dB.
The trained model depends strongly on exit alignment. The small repair
benefit motivates a matched-retraining and timing comparison before
increasing its capacity. These interventions measure dependence of fixed
weights, not the best performance attainable without each module.

\paragraph{Stream-only reconstruction.}
A separate CPU decoder receives the common entropy payload and explicit
two-bit exit indices. On two predeclared QP32 frames with four maps each,
all eight images, latents, symbols and entropy indexes match encoder-side
reconstruction exactly; inner entropy bytes stay identical across maps.
This verifies the shared-payload path on those cases. Map/header bytes and
malformed-stream checks are in the supplement; native CUDA interoperability
and full-cohort timing remain unmeasured.

\subsection{What the encoder pays for a stopping decision}

Source-informed search needs candidate reconstructions to populate its error
table. The archived QP32 benchmark records 1,023.26\,ms for four distinct
candidate outputs and 336.87\,ms for a shared tiled pass. These upper
medians pool 24 trials over eight frames; raw timing distributions and
device load were not retained. They indicate the cost of acquiring decision
information, not an end-to-end speedup. The supplement details the stages.

A fixed-control predictor can avoid this table, but the evaluated
source-calibrated router cannot: its per-frame $\beta$ uses the same error
information. The next latency experiment must compare full decision paths,
including feature generation, calibration, signalling, conditional execution
and final verification. At a loose quality budget, where the router and blind
maps nearly coincide, even small additional overhead may remove the practical
benefit of content prediction.
