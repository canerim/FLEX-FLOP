\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Consider two regions decoded from the same image. In one, another pair of
synthesis blocks improves reconstruction; in the other, it changes little.
A fixed-depth decoder pays for both. Efficient operators make this work
cheaper, but leave the allocation unchanged. Spatial early exit asks a
complementary question: \emph{where is further decoding worth its cost?}

We investigate this question in the intra model supplied with
DCVC-UF~\cite{li2026dcvcuf}. Its synthesis transform contains twelve
constant-width blocks between upsampling and reconstruction, providing a
concrete axis along which to vary capacity. We turn this fixed stack into
an image-dependent allocation of depth. Following the incremental-benefit
view of adaptive patch exiting~\cite{wang2022ape}, a useful decision reflects
what additional processing can recover, rather than texture alone. In a
codec, that benefit also depends on the information preserved at the chosen
rate: a visually complex region need not justify the deepest reconstruction.

There are two natural ways to make this decision
(\cref{fig:overview}). \emph{Across models}, a selector assigns each patch
to a codec of suitable capacity, following the patch-to-network principle
of ClassSR~\cite{kong2021classsr}. \emph{Within a model}, different patches
leave a shared synthesis stack at different depths. The distinction is
where the commitment is made. An independent codec is chosen before
encoding and can learn its own representation; an exit is chosen after
one representation has been recovered and reuses its synthesis prefix.
FLEX-UF implements and evaluates the latter. The six-depth bank is a
complementary study of how specialisation trades against this reuse.

Inside a codec, early exit requires more than attaching intermediate
outputs. Features at different depths must support a common reconstruction;
conditional tiles lose some spatial context; and choosing an exit can itself
require expensive candidate decoding. FLEX-UF addresses execution with a
full-frame stem, a shrinking batch of active tiles, pointwise exit adapters,
and a shared repair and reconstruction head. Exited tiles leave the active
batch, making the conditional path an actual reduction in executed blocks.
The frozen analysis and entropy path supplies one common latent; its
payload can support different stopping maps without re-encoding the image.

We separate the value of \emph{cheaper depth} from the value of
\emph{content-dependent placement}. Uniform depth tests global truncation.
Ordered dithering permits mixtures of depths without predicting where
extra synthesis is useful. Under source-calibrated control, learned routing
improves on this stronger baseline by \MainPremium\ percentage points of
synthesis MAC saving at a nominal 0.1\,dB target, above dithering's
\MainDitherSaving\% reduction. That increment shrinks near the shallowest-exit
ceiling. Adaptation has most room to help when extra depth still matters
but does not matter equally everywhere. A common delivered-loss analysis
retains this pattern. A separate QP32 replay fixes controls on other
sequences; its paired routing margin remains statistically unresolved.
This separates evidence for useful allocation from evidence that a
source-independent stopping control reliably captures that value.

We contribute a spatially conditional DCVC-UF synthesis path and a
controlled evaluation of learned placement. Uniform depth, blind mixtures,
a common measured-loss cap and fixed-control replay distinguish the
architecture's saving from the selector's value. Frozen-weight interventions
and a stream-only decoding check probe the mechanism. The independent-depth
study extends this allocation question to codec selection; complete codec
speedups remain a question for measured execution.
