\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Efficient learned decoding.}
DCVC-RT argues that arithmetic count alone misses important operational
costs, including memory traffic and execution overhead~\cite{jia2025dcvcrt}.
DCVC-UF advances throughput-oriented codec design~\cite{li2026dcvcuf}.
Our intervention is confined to the synthesis transform of its intra model:
the representation and entropy path are shared, and computation varies
spatially after entropy recovery. Shallow-decoder work offers a complementary
approach by reducing synthesis capacity globally~\cite{yang2023shallow}.
This motivates treating a fixed shallow decoder as a necessary control for
an adaptive one, rather than attributing every reduction from full depth to
successful routing.

\paragraph{Spatial adaptation and early exit.}
ClassSR assigns super-resolution patches to networks of different
capacity~\cite{kong2021classsr}, whereas APE predicts incremental benefit to
select each patch's exit depth~\cite{wang2022ape}. RBQE adapts
the depth used to enhance already compressed images~\cite{xing2020rbqe}.
These studies establish adaptive reconstruction effort. FLEX-UF
applies this principle inside DCVC-UF synthesis, where a common coded
representation, exit-feature alignment and tile-boundary context constrain
the available stopping paths. Our evaluation also distinguishes the benefit
of spatial placement from the cheaper average depth it enables.

\paragraph{Adaptive capacity within compression.}
Slimmable compressive autoencoders vary model
width~\cite{yang2021slimcae}; AdaNIC varies transform width spatially using
blockwise operators and a learned router~\cite{tao2023adanic}; cgSlimDecoder controls decoder
channel widths under complexity constraints~\cite{hu2023cgslim}. These
methods already demonstrate adaptive computation in learned compression.
Our focus is nested depth in DCVC-UF, executed through a shared stem and a
shrinking tile batch. We do not claim that content-dependent codec
computation itself is new.

\paragraph{Complete-codec selection.}
Spatial competition trains content-specialized codecs, compares their
rate--distortion costs by region and communicates a mode
map~\cite{blard2026spatial}. Its mode search evaluates multiple complete
codecs at the encoder. Our planned depth bank instead varies synthesis
capacity and tests a predictor that selects a codec before encoding.
Each expert still owns its analysis and entropy parameters, so selection
changes both representation and rate. This is a separate capacity frontier,
not additional exits of the present shared-latent checkpoint.
