\section{Spatially Adaptive DCVC-UF}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The allocation problem}
Let $\mathbf a=(a_1,\ldots,a_N)$ assign a synthesis option to each
region. A common formulation is
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\mathbf a}\; \mathcal C(\mathbf a)
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad
 D(x,\hat x_{\mathbf a})-D(x,\hat x_{\rm ref})\leq\delta,
 \label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
where $D$ is a distortion measure and $\mathcal C$ is the chosen compute
cost. In our PSNR-loss experiments, the constraint uses the corresponding
logarithmic ratio of reconstruction MSEs. The distortion belongs to the
\emph{assembled image}: spatial interactions can invalidate an additive
tile-error approximation.

For early exit, $a_t$ identifies a stopping point after one latent has been
recovered. For the prospective model bank, $a_t$ identifies a complete
codec before encoding, making both payload rate and reconstruction depend
on the assignment. An independent-bank comparison must therefore also
match rate, including expert signalling. Equation~\eqref{eq:allocation}
organises the two designs; it does not assert that their feasible sets or
costs are equal. We implement the shared-exit case below and specify the
independent-capacity experiment in \cref{sec:depth}.

\subsection{A shared representation, a variable synthesis depth}
Let $\hat y$ be the recovered latent of the fixed DCVC-UF analysis transform
and entropy model. The synthesis network upsamples it by two and processes
384-channel features at one eighth of the RGB resolution. We divide its
twelve trunk blocks into six consecutive pairs. A split index determines
which pairs run on the full frame and which run conditionally on tiles.
In the evaluated configuration, four blocks form the common stem and the
remaining eight form a four-stage conditional suffix.

The stem output is partitioned into $32\times32$ feature tiles, each
covering $256\times256$ RGB pixels. For a tile assigned depth $k$, let
$f_t^{(k)}$ be its feature after the corresponding block pair and let
$A_k$ be its adapter. The complete image is reconstructed as
\begin{equation}
 \hat x_{\mathbf k}=H\!\left(q\odot G\!\left[
 \operatorname{stitch}_t A_{k_t}(f_t^{(k_t)})\right]\right),
 \label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
where $G$ is grid-conditioned repair, $q$ is the decoder's quality scaling,
and $H$ is the common output head, including pixel shuffle. Adaptation acts
on features before the shared head, so tiles with different stopping depths
need not be stitched directly in RGB space.

\paragraph{Reachable depth and actual execution.}
Although the configuration contains six nominal exit indices, the first two
precede the split and are clamped in this evaluation. The distinct reachable
depths are $\mathcal K=\{6,8,10,12\}$. At each conditional stage the
implementation gathers only the surviving tiles, executes the next block
pair, and writes departing tiles through their adapters. A deeper tile
reuses all computation already performed. Evaluating all four candidates
and masking their RGB outputs would not implement this execution saving.
For the recorded example in \cref{fig:system}, the active population decreases
from 40 to 27, seven and three tiles.

\subsection{Exit adaptation and boundary consistency}

An intermediate feature is not automatically compatible with the final head.
The exit adapter supplies the missing transformation with pointwise residual
operators. Its residual output is zero-initialised, preserving the inherited
feature at initialisation. The evaluated depth-scaled adapters allocate
capacity according to the depth they replace. Pointwise operators avoid
introducing a new spatial receptive field at each exit; their arithmetic is
included in the per-exit cost model. The deepest exit uses an identity
connection rather than an additional learned adapter.

Independent tile execution still removes context from the trunk's spatial
operators. The conditional trunk uses replicate padding without a halo.
After stitching, a depthwise spatial filter and pointwise mixing correct the
full feature canvas. A learned gate indexed by position within the tile
gives the repair access to the known boundary lattice. The head then operates
on the complete canvas. This design spends a shared output-stage cost to
recover boundary context instead of expanding every conditional block with
overlapping halos. Whether that trade is favourable is an empirical question:
repair removal, halo size and patch size belong in separate matched
ablations, with retraining distinguished from an inference-only intervention.

\subsection{Training the exits and the stopping predictor}

The shared-latent model is initialised from released DCVC-UF weights.
Analysis and entropy parameters remain frozen; the synthesis network,
adapters and repair are fine-tuned. Training samples tile depths and applies
reconstruction supervision through the mixed tiled path. Thus the adapters
learn under the same stitching and full-frame head used at inference. The
training implementation also supports a full-depth reconstruction anchor
against the frozen release and adjacent-exit feature distillation.
The inspected e15 training configuration enables both terms. Its objective can be written as
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\mathbb E_{\mathbf k}\!\left[R+\lambda D(x,\hat x_{\mathbf k})\right]
 +a\mathcal L_{\rm anchor}+b\mathcal L_{\rm feature}.
 \label{eq:training}
\end{equation}
Here the latent-rate term $R$ is shared across exits. With the front end
frozen it does not optimise exit-dependent rate. The archived fine-tuning
configuration uses 512-pixel crops and batch size eight; it is distinct from
the new full 105-epoch official-recipe depth study in \cref{sec:depth}.
The supplement records the available training provenance and its limits.

The evaluated stopping predictor uses a 384-to-48 pointwise stem projection,
per-tile mean and standard deviation, and the normalised QP. A two-layer
256-unit MLP predicts exit scores. Inspection of the actual router checkpoint
shows that its live inputs are \emph{stem and QP}; the more general
implementation contains latent, entropy-scale and bit-feature branches, but
these do not supply content information in this run. This distinction is
important for reproducing the model rather than merely its class name.

For tile errors $M_{t,k}$ and normalised costs $c_k$, the training target is
softened from the Lagrangian $L_{t,k}=M_{t,k}+\lambda_c c_k$:
\begin{equation}
 p^*_{t,k}\propto\exp\!\left[-\frac{L_{t,k}-\min_j L_{t,j}}
 {\tau\,\max(\max_j L_{t,j}-\min_j L_{t,j},\epsilon)}\right].
 \label{eq:softtarget}
\end{equation}
The checkpoint records $\lambda_c=1.3\times10^{-5}$ and $\tau=0.1$.
Soft-label cross-entropy, together with the implementation's regret term,
reduces the penalty for confusing nearly equivalent exits. This loss trains
an allocation predictor; it is not a calibrated bound on final image error.

\subsection{Stopping under a quality target}

For equal padded tiles, the arithmetic model is
\begin{equation}
 C(\mathbf k)=\frac1N\sum_t c_{k_t},\qquad
 S(\mathbf k)=100[1-C(\mathbf k)].
 \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
Shared stem and output work, exit adapters and conditional depth are included
in $c_k$. Entropy-recovery networks, arithmetic coding, router execution,
signalling and system overhead are outside this synthesis MAC quantity.
The four costs are approximately
$\{0.609,0.758,0.870,1.010\}$ relative to full-frame synthesis. In particular,
a tiled all-deep map can cost more than the full-frame reference because repair
is still executed.

Source-informed search minimises $M_{t,k}+\lambda c_k$ per tile and adjusts
$\lambda$ for a target table loss. The learned alternative uses
\begin{equation}
 k_t(\beta)=\arg\max_k\{\ell_{t,k}-\beta c_k\},
 \label{eq:router}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ denotes log scores. Uniform depth selects one exit per frame.
Bayer dithering mixes adjacent exits with a fixed spatial ordering. Its
mixture fraction is calibrated under the same target, so it controls for
finer average depth without predicting spatial difficulty.

In the recorded sweep, all controls are calibrated per source frame using
stored error tables. This is an attainable allocation study, not a test of
autonomous held-out calibration. Increasing $\beta$ cannot increase selected
cost, but source distortion need not be monotone; the supplement audits the
finite assignment path for both router and dither.

\subsection{Quality references and decision information}

The table target compares padded RGB candidate errors to a released-weight
full-depth reference. Final reported loss instead compares the cropped mixed
output to the cropped, fine-tuned e15 full-frame output. These anchors are
not interchangeable: the encoder/entropy weights match, while inherited
synthesis weights have changed. Full-frame repair also couples neighbouring
tiles. A table threshold therefore does not guarantee the same numerical
loss after final reconstruction. We retain this distinction in the nominal
sweep and add a retrospective common-reference delivered-loss comparison.

The information path also determines the deployment cost. Encoder-selected
maps require side information. A source-calibrated router needs its control
parameter communicated or reproducibly recovered. A decoder-local router
with a fixed validation-selected control can avoid a per-frame source table,
but its quality--cost frontier must be measured separately. Common latents
establish a shared payload opportunity; they do not make unmeasured routing
headers or complete bitstreams free.
