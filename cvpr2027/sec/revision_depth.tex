\section{From Shared Exits to Independent Depths}
\label{sec:depth}

Shared early exit uses one coded representation. Our complementary
2/4/6/8/10/12-block DCVC-UF bank instead allows each codec to learn
its own analysis and entropy model (\cref{fig:bank-system}). D2/D4 have
completed training; D6/D8/D10 and scratch D12 are not final here. No
six-expert routing result is claimed.

\paragraph{A controlled capacity frontier.}
The scratch models follow the official 105-epoch recipe on all 384,795
Open Images train\_0/1/2 images~\cite{microsoft2026training}. At fixed
DIV2K epoch-20/30 milestones, 2,000 actual bitstreams per milestone
show D6's RGB advantage over D2 at 0.2 payload bpp changing from
0.183 to 0.107\,dB; D6 versus D4 at epoch 30 is unresolved.
A separate final Kodak evaluation at 0.2 \emph{estimated} bpp finds D4
0.118\,dB [0.095, 0.143] above D2 on 24 paired images. Released D12
adds 0.155\,dB [0.126, 0.186] over D4, but has a different training
history. The supplement gives YUV results, rate sweeps and protocol
limits. Same-recipe D12 and matched shared-exit training are needed
to isolate depth and weight sharing.

\paragraph{The spatial cost of specialisation.}
Patchwise expert selection also changes geometry and entropy boundaries.
With identical D6 weights, independently coded regions lose quality at
matched rate; increasing context alone does not remove their rate cost.
In a separate fixed D2/D6 assignment, merging compatible neighbouring
regions improves matched-rate quality while retaining the same per-pixel
depth map. These controls, detailed in the supplement, make patchwise D12
and blind expert mixtures essential baselines before a selector receives
credit. The eventual comparison must match achieved rate and quality,
charge signalling and grouping, and measure the effect of expert occupancy
on latency. It will test when independent specialisation is worth more
than shared-prefix reuse.
