\section{From Shared Exits to Model Selection}
\label{sec:depth}

The early-exit result raises a second allocation question: how much
reconstruction quality does sharing a representation and synthesis prefix
cost compared with specialising each capacity? Our next study compares
independently trained DCVC-UF codecs retaining 2/4/6/8/10/12 synthesis
blocks. Following ClassSR's patch-to-network principle~\cite{kong2021classsr},
a later selector will route each patch to one member of this bank. Their encoder and entropy parameters also
learn, so their latents are not interchangeable exits of the current model.
\Cref{tab:depth} reports exact capacity and experimental status; completed
matched RD and latency results are pending.

Each variant preserves the opening upsampler and output head. D2 removes
73.0\% of D12's synthesis parameters but only 24.6\% of complete-codec
parameters, because analysis and entropy capacity remain. A same-recipe
D12 trained from scratch must complement the released anchor to isolate
depth from training provenance. Likewise, attributing a gap between this
bank and the older e15 fine-tuning run specifically to weight sharing
requires matched data, initialisation and optimisation controls.

\paragraph{Training and evaluation.}
The independent runs retain the pinned official 105-epoch
recipe~\cite{microsoft2026training}, all 384,795 Open Images train\_0/1/2
images, batch size 16 and FP32. The supplement records the schedule and
execution settings. Four validation crops monitor health; final comparison
requires common achieved RD, complete coded bytes and paired timing.
Equal QP is not equal rate across codecs.

\paragraph{Specialisation changes the decision path.}
Unlike shared exits, separately trained codecs may learn different analysis
transforms and entropy distributions. Choosing D4 after encoding with D12
is therefore not a valid substitute for encoding with D4. The selector must
act on cheap source-side features before the chosen codec runs; its training
target should reflect reconstruction benefit, coded rate and measured cost,
rather than a texture label alone. This is the compression-specific
qualification to the ClassSR analogy.

\paragraph{Later encoder-side selection.}
The proposed MLP in the upper path of \cref{fig:overview} selects an expert before patch
encoding and transmits its identity. Controls are full-frame D12,
patchwise D12, a validation-selected fixed expert and blind mixtures.
Leave-one-expert-out tests measure patch-specific value. Training,
calibration and test images stay separate; timing includes grouping,
entropy resets, signalling and fallback. The supplement's dashed runtime
scenarios expose assumed fixed costs without inventing quality or speed
measurements for unfinished models.

\paragraph{A budget is also a scheduling problem.}
Expert latency depends on the number of assigned patches and their packing,
not just on nominal model size. The pinned native codec allocates batch-one
buffers; batched expert execution needs additional implementation. The
supplement specifies a prospective grouped-runtime objective. Comparing
shared exits with independent experts must charge entropy work, selection,
transfers and assembly at matched achieved rate and quality.
