\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The architecture of a learned codec fixes synthesis depth before it sees
a particular image. In the DCVC-UF intra model~\cite{li2026dcvcuf},
every spatial location
traverses the same twelve synthesis blocks, although additional blocks
need not improve every location equally. This uniform schedule makes
decoder capacity easy to provision, but ties the cost of an entire frame
to the deepest path. We ask a narrower question than whether the
decoder can be made smaller: \emph{can its existing capacity be spent
where it improves a particular decoded image?}

Spatially adaptive computation has clear precedents. ClassSR sends
super-resolution patches to networks of different sizes
\cite{kong2021classsr}; APE instead predicts when a patch should leave
a shared restoration network~\cite{wang2022ape}. In compression,
AdaNIC already routes spatial regions through dynamic transforms
\cite{tao2023adanic}. These works motivate allocation, but they do not
answer the decoder-depth question in DCVC-UF. The decoder receives a
\emph{coded} latent: texture visible in the source is useful only if
the bitstream preserves information that further synthesis can recover.
Moreover, the chosen path must truly skip computation while returning
tiles to one coherent image. An exit selected after evaluating every
block would have no decoding benefit.

We study two distinct ways to expose this capacity axis under the name
\textbf{RegLIC} (\cref{fig:bank-system,fig:system}). The evaluated system
shares one analysis transform, bitstream and decoder prefix. A tile
continues through conditional block pairs only while it remains active;
an exit adapter maps its features to a common space, and a full-frame
repair and reconstruction head assembles the result. A complementary
bank of independently trained 2/4/6/8/10/12-block codecs would route
\emph{before} encoding, allowing the representation and entropy model
to specialise with depth. That bank changes the rate and boundary
problem as well as the compute budget. We therefore keep its training
study separate from the measured shared-exit results and do not claim
six-way routing performance here.

The crucial comparison is between \emph{using fewer blocks} and
\emph{placing} the remaining blocks well. Across 53 intra frames and
five quality indices, source-calibrated shared-exit routing saves
\MainRouterSaving\% of modelled synthesis MACs at a nominal
0.1\,dB YCbCr-MSE-ratio target. A non-content-dependent depth mixture
accounts for much of this saving; the extra benefit of learned
placement is smaller and varies with the quality budget. On the QP32
fixed-control replay, that paired benefit is not yet resolved. These
results support conditional execution and measured arithmetic savings,
while leaving source-independent routing gains and full-codec latency
as separate questions.

\noindent\textbf{Overall, our contributions are three-fold:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item We formulate spatial synthesis-depth allocation for DCVC-UF and
distinguish a shared-latent exit from a bank of independently coded
depths, making their different rate and boundary costs explicit.
\item We realise genuine tile-wise early exit: inactive tiles skip later
block pairs, and depth-specific adapters feed a common full-frame
repair and reconstruction path.
\item We evaluate the executed-depth/quality trade-off against uniform
and non-content-dependent allocations, with calibration controls that
separate arithmetic savings from the still-unresolved placement gain.
\end{enumerate}
