\begin{abstract}
The benefit of another decoder layer varies across an image, yet DCVC-UF
applies the same synthesis depth everywhere. We introduce FLEX-UF, a
spatial early-exit extension of its intra model. A shared coded
representation and synthesis prefix feed tiles that stop at different
depths. Exit adapters assemble their features for one full-frame repair
and reconstruction head; only surviving tiles execute later blocks.
We evaluate two distinct sources of efficiency: reducing average depth
and choosing where to retain it. Across 53 intra frames at five quality
indices, learned routing saves \MainRouterSaving\% of modelled synthesis
multiply--accumulates at a nominal 0.1\,dB YCbCr-MSE target under
per-source calibration. Of this saving, \MainPremium\ percentage points
exceed ordered dithering, which mixes depths without predicting their
spatial value. Retrospective selection under a common measured-loss cap
retains a 2.93-point margin over all 265 pairs. This margin contracts as
the shallowest exit saturates. Fixing controls on other sequences removes
per-source calibration, but a QP32 reconstruction replay does not yet
resolve the paired routing advantage. These results identify where
adaptive depth can add value while separating arithmetic savings from
unmeasured codec latency. An ongoing independent-depth study extends
the same allocation question from shared exits to codec selection.
\end{abstract}
