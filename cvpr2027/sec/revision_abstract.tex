\begin{abstract}
A fast learned codec still spends the same synthesis depth on every image
region. We introduce FLEX-UF, a spatial early-exit design for the intra
decoder of DCVC-UF that makes this depth a local decision. A shared latent
and full-frame stem feed a nested decoder; tiles leave through pointwise
adapters, while a shared repair and reconstruction head restore a complete
image. The design reuses computation across exits and preserves the learned
representation. We study source-informed selection and learned routing
against uniform depth and ordered dithering on 53 intra frames at five
quality indices. At a nominal 0.1\,dB target, the router removes
\MainRouterSaving\% of modelled decoder multiply--accumulates, including
\MainPremium\ percentage points beyond dithering. A retrospective comparison
under a common RGB-loss cap against fine-tuned full-depth synthesis retains
a 2.93-point margin across all
265 frame--quality pairs. The benefit contracts as the shallowest exit
saturates, identifying when spatial adaptation is useful. These results
establish an arithmetic--quality trade-off under source-calibrated control;
complete bitstream latency remains to be measured. A controlled
2/4/6/8/10/12-block study is specified to test how shared early exits compare
with independently trained depth-reduced DCVC-UF codecs.
\end{abstract}
