\begin{abstract}
Learned image codecs usually apply one decoder depth to an entire image,
although the benefit of additional synthesis varies across regions.
We introduce \textbf{RegLIC}, a region-adaptive depth framework studied
with the DCVC-UF intra codec. Its evaluated shared-exit path recovers one
latent, executes later synthesis blocks only on surviving tiles, and
assembles mixed-depth features through adapters and a common
reconstruction head. Across 53 intra frames and five quality indices,
learned routing saves \MainRouterSaving\% of modelled synthesis MACs at
a nominal 0.1\,dB YCbCr-MSE-ratio target under per-source calibration;
\MainPremium\ percentage points exceed ordered dithering, which mixes
depths without content-dependent placement. A retrospective common
delivered-loss cap preserves a routing margin, whereas a fixed-control
QP32 replay does not yet resolve its paired advantage. We also define a
complementary 2/4/6/8/10/12-block codec bank to study independent
representation specialisation; its router is not part of the reported
results. These controls distinguish savings from shallower decoding,
spatial allocation, and the still-unmeasured effect on full-codec latency.
\end{abstract}
