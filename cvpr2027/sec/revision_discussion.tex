\section{Discussion and Next Experiments}
\label{sec:discussion}

The relevant deployment question is whether content prediction improves the
rate--distortion--time frontier after its own costs are included. The current
paired analysis identifies a region of budgets where further routing work is
plausible and a saturation region where even an accurate predictor has little
room to help. It also gives strong controls: uniform depth, ordered dithering,
and source-informed selection should accompany every router comparison.

Three experimental gaps are decisive. First, quality must be verified on the
final cropped reconstruction in the chosen metric, with a measured fallback
for infeasible or over-budget plans. Second, a held-out, decoder-available
control policy must be compared separately from test-frame source calibration.
Third, actual encoded bytes and end-to-end timings must include decision
signalling, entropy decoding, routing, tile movement and stitching, measured
on the same device with paired execution orders.

An ongoing depth study trains independent DCVC-UF intra codecs with two,
four and six decoder trunk blocks under the pinned official training recipe.
Their encoders and entropy models are also trained, so their latents are not
interchangeable exits of the shared-latent model studied here. Released D12
is a deployment reference; a D12 model trained under the same protocol is
needed to isolate depth from training provenance. No incomplete training
snapshot is included as a final result in this manuscript.

\section{Conclusion}
Spatial early exit provides a concrete mechanism for reducing decoder
arithmetic, but its scientific value is the gain beyond cheaper blind
allocations. On the recorded shared-latent DCVC-UF experiment, that gain is
largest in a restricted quality-budget range and contracts as the ladder
saturates. Delivered-quality checks and the cost of acquiring decision
information are equally central to the claim. Together, these observations
define a testable route from a flexible decoder to a practical adaptive codec.
