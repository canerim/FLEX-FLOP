\section{Tracing the Reconstruction Reference}

Two measurement paths need to be distinguished. The source-table producer
loads the e15 network and a separate released-weight reference. It computes
the candidate tile errors $M_{t,k}$ from e15 and the scalar reference error
$R$ from the released synthesis network, both on the padded source.
The final-reconstruction evaluator instead loads e15 alone, calls its
full-frame synthesis path, crops both full and mixed outputs, and measures
their difference in source quality. Its raw field named
\texttt{psnr\_release} is consequently a fine-tuned full-depth reference
in the inspected code (\cref{fig:anchors}).

\paragraph{Metric provenance.}
The legacy field \texttt{db\_rgb} is also misnamed. The CTC reader upsamples
YCbCr~4:2:0 to 4:4:4 and centres the three planes. Synthesis returns this
representation, and the evaluator computes equal-channel tensor MSE
directly, before RGB conversion or clipping. Both the padded selection
tables and the cropped MSE-ratio loss therefore use YCbCr~4:4:4.
We label this quantity $\Delta_{444}$ or ``444-MSE loss'' throughout.
The separately recorded 6:1:1 metric is a weighted average of plane PSNRs
after chroma downsampling and clipping, not the same MSE ratio.

Two fixed-map CPU replays with strict checkpoint loading reproduce the
archived \texttt{db\_rgb} values within $1.1\times10^{-6}$\,dB
(\cref{fig:metricproof}). Converting to RGB changes the result. These checks
establish the interpretation of the inspected path; they are not a new
265-pair RGB evaluation. Legacy JSON keys and historical annotations are
preserved for traceability; \texttt{METRICS.md} records their corrected
meaning and the replay hashes. The separate independent-depth validation
explicitly converts reconstructions to RGB and must not be pooled with
this legacy metric.

A CPU comparison of the two state dictionaries finds 255 shared
encoder/entropy tensors with no changes. All 143 inherited synthesis tensors

After exact key remapping, all 398 inherited warm-start tensors also match
the archived official D12 release. Extra adapter/router tensors are outside
this comparison. This second check ties the inspected warm start to the
released parameters without assuming that its filename proves provenance.

This distinction limits the interpretation of threshold crossings. The
recorded 7/263 YCbCr~4:4:4 and 49/263 YUV losses above a nominal 0.1\,dB target combine
differences in reference, image support, metric and the influence of the
shared repair/head. They do not identify the contribution of any single
factor. The paired MAC accounting remains attached to the same recorded
maps, but the exact quality gap to the cropped released decoder cannot be
recovered from the exported JSON alone.

The completed QP32 replay in \cref{sec:releasedanchor} retains both
full-depth anchors and explicitly computes valid-pixel RGB errors on all
53 frames. It closes this reference gap at that QP without relabelling the
five-QP archive. Selection and final verification must still declare the
same reference and metric; any deployment fallback must include its bytes
and runtime.

\section{What Additional Synthesis Depth Buys}

Each column of the archived source-error table was produced by decoding a
uniform-depth map and measuring per-tile source MSE. Its equal-area tile
average is therefore the padded YCbCr~4:4:4 MSE of that actual uniform
reconstruction. Comparing this mean to the separately evaluated released
reference gives mean losses of 0.2045, 0.0968, 0.0605 and 0.0379\,dB for
6/8/10/12 blocks over all 265 pairs. The corresponding modelled MAC savings
are 39.12\%, 24.22\%, 13.03\% and $-0.95$\%. The negative all-deep saving
reflects shared repair overhead relative to the full-frame reference.
These values use padded support and cannot substitute for cropped
released-reference replay.

For adjacent depths, the local quality improvement is
$g_t=10\log_{10}(M_{t,k}/M_{t,k+1})$. The frame-median improvement, averaged
across pairs, is 0.0993\,dB for 6-to-8 blocks, 0.0320\,dB for 8-to-10,
and 0.0213\,dB for 10-to-12. The respective mean within-frame interquartile
ranges are 0.0748, 0.0340 and 0.0238\,dB. This quantifies both diminishing
returns and variation within the same image. The average fractions of tiles
whose error increases are 4.37\%, 11.55\% and 11.41\%; deeper synthesis is
not uniformly better at every location.

\Cref{fig:exitprofile} uses equal frame--QP weighting, rather than letting
larger images dominate through their tile counts. Sequence-cluster intervals
retain all five QPs together. Each tile comparison still changes the depth
of its neighbours because the underlying maps are uniform. It is a
conditional reconstruction diagnostic, not a causal one-tile intervention
or a completed independent-codec depth ablation.

\section{Independent-Image Calibration of the Decoder-Side Control}

We next fit the scalar price without using Kodak records in the optimisation.
The 100 original DIV2K validation PNGs are ordered by the SHA256 of their
filenames. The first 24 supply calibration; the next, disjoint 24 supply an
internal transfer check. Every image contributes a central $768\times512$
RGB crop, encoded into actual FUFREF2 streams at QP 0/16/32/48/63.
The released analysis and entropy tensors are shared with the e15 model.
The same decoder-side stem--QP router, six-tile assembly, seam repair,
full-frame e15 reconstruction and cropped YCbCr~4:4:4 metric are used at
every candidate price. This is an untimed, single-core CPU replay.

The candidate grid, fixed before evaluation, contains prices from $-50$ to
$60$ in increments of five, plus the exact earlier CTC price at each QP
and the exact QP16 price. For each QP separately, the primary rule maximises
mean analytical synthesis MAC saving among candidates whose mean
$\Delta_{444}$ on the 24 calibration images is at most 0.1\,dB.
It selects $\beta=(40,15,15,10,-10)$ at increasing QP. Mean saving/loss
is 20.73\%/0.0851\,dB on calibration, 20.05\%/0.0889\,dB on held-out
DIV2K, and 25.88\%/0.0976\,dB on Kodak24$\times$QP5. On Kodak, the
24-image cluster-bootstrap 95\% intervals are [23.29,28.21]\% MAC saving
and [0.0832,0.1128]\,dB mean $\Delta_{444}$. The earlier CTC and fixed-QP16
controls, evaluated on the same Kodak streams, yield 17.16\%/0.0565\,dB
and 26.82\%/0.1223\,dB, respectively.

The pooled target is not a hard guarantee. Under the primary policy,
47/120 Kodak cases exceed 0.1\,dB; QP48/63 means are 0.1275/0.1261\,dB,
with 17/24 and 15/24 cases above the threshold. The largest Kodak loss is

candidate exactly. These tails motivate a risk-aware control rather than
an unqualified quality-preservation claim.

After inspecting the calibration aggregate, we also explored a less
conservative rule: maximise five-QP mean saving subject only to five-QP mean
calibration loss $\leq0.1$\,dB. Its price vector
$(50,15,15,14.0229,5)$ reaches 23.09\%/0.09994\,dB on calibration, but

Thus the apparent target hit does not transfer. The rule was selected using
calibration alone and committed before Kodak replay, but it was introduced
after inspecting that cohort and is therefore labelled exploratory.
Kodak had been examined in prior diagnostics; it is independent of these
specific price fits, not globally untouched. A fresh cohort is needed for
a final Q90 or maximum-loss-constrained policy comparison.

\paragraph{The router is not the only source of tail loss.}
On the 120 DIV2K calibration cases, forcing every tile to the deepest exit
still exceeds 0.1\,dB in five cases: every QP of image 0828. Its all-deep
tiled losses are 0.115/0.164/0.152/0.144/0.145\,dB at increasing QP.
No scalar price on the frozen candidate grid can meet the threshold for
those cases. This is a tiled-versus-full-frame floor, not proof that every
possible spatial map or decoder architecture has the same floor.
Direct uncached replay exactly reproduces the QP0 primary-map loss of
0.7918\,dB and all-deep floor of 0.1148\,dB. A 16-pixel band at the internal
tile boundaries occupies 13.64\% of pixels but contains 72.43\% of the
all-deep/full-frame squared difference energy; its mean difference is
16.6 times the interior. The structural floor and the router-dependent
excess therefore require separate controls (\cref{fig:beta-floor}).

An outcome-informed oracle restricted to the same finite scalar-price grid
reduces the calibration threshold failures from 34 to those five cases,
while saving 21.60\% mean synthesis MAC versus the fixed primary policy's
20.73\%. It consults source reconstruction quality and is not deployable.
Of the primary policy's 34 calibration violations, 29 are below threshold
under tiled all-deep decoding; an ideal source-informed binary fallback
would remove those 29 but lower mean saving to 13.48\%. The candidate-grid
oracle retains 21.60\% because it chooses cheaper feasible maps rather
than always sending a risky frame to all-deep. This gap is a motivation for
a decoder-available risk signal, not a measured router result.
As a first negative control, we fitted a fixed L2 logistic risk score from
decoder-visible QP and six-tile exit-map summaries on the 24 calibration
images, then evaluated once on the disjoint 24-image DIV2K set. Its
violation AUC drops from 0.785 to 0.646, slightly below the 0.651 AUC of
mean exit depth alone. A calibration-locked threshold flags 74/120
validation cases yet misses 7/35 violations. Map geometry alone is not a
reliable quality guard in this pilot; additional decoder-available evidence
and a fresh cohort are required before proposing a fallback policy.
The unchanged score ranks Kodak24$\times$QP5 cases better (AUC 0.779), but
the same threshold flags 97/120 cases to catch 44/47 violations. Kodak was
not used in this fit, although it had been inspected in earlier project
diagnostics; this is a transfer check, not a pristine external holdout.
Conversely, a fixed per-QP Q90 calibration rule reduces mean saving to
11.20\% on these calibration cases. These are explanatory calibration
ablations, not external transfer results. They show why simply tightening
the scalar price may sacrifice much of the compute gain without repairing
the tiled reconstruction floor.

\paragraph{A context counterfactual separates the two failure mechanisms.}
For image 0828 we replayed the same five research bitstreams with the fixed
router maps and two \emph{diagnostic} suffix implementations: clipped feature
windows wide enough to supply every depthwise layer, and a canvas that shares
only one-pixel context at each depthwise layer. Both disable the originally
trained seam-repair module. With all tiles at the deepest exit, each produces
the full-frame e15 output exactly at every QP (maximum absolute difference
zero). Thus the tiled all-deep floor is removable by restoring true context;
it is not an inherent penalty of the checkpoint. For the frozen mixed map,
QP0 still loses 0.720\,dB with exact context because its selected exits are
mostly shallow. At QP16--63, the same intervention reduces the 0.153--0.172\,dB
deployed losses to 0.011--0.016\,dB, isolating the tile boundary as the
dominant cause (\cref{fig:context-counterfactual}).

Exact windows compute the entire costly suffix over expanded areas. Their
analytical synthesis cost is 1.315 times full-frame e15 for the QP16--63
mixed maps, compared with 0.968 for the deployed zero-halo path; neither
number measures latency. Cropping one invalid context
ring after each suffix block is output-identical to the full-window method
on 0828/QP63, and lowers its mixed-map synthesis cost to 1.151 times full
e15; this still exceeds the full decode. An optimistic union-of-required-cells
estimate, which shares all overlapping tile context at zero scheduling cost,
is 0.965 times full e15 on that same map: exact quality leaves only 3.5\%
ideal synthesis saving there. This is an area calculation, not a kernel or
latency result. Depthwise-only coupling gives
0.056--0.099\,dB on these four QPs without haloing pointwise layers, but
the mixed-depth boundary
still differs from a true full-depth neighbour. Its PyTorch implementation
also assembles and gathers halo buffers, so low arithmetic overhead is not
evidence of low wall-clock overhead. Retaining the old seam-repair weights
can worsen the coupled result (QP63: 0.149 versus 0.099\,dB); a coupled
decoder is a new variant requiring broader quality and matched latency tests.
Indeed, two post-hoc validation stress cases give a counterexample to
deploying coupling as a global switch. On 0898/QP48, four of six tiles are
deepest, yet coupling without repair raises loss from 0.165 to 0.365\,dB
against an otherwise matched zero-padding/no-repair control. On the shallow
0844/QP48 map it barely changes loss (0.573 to 0.572\,dB). These cases were
selected after inspecting validation failures; they establish a failure
mode, not its frequency. The mixed-depth neighbour state needs a new
boundary rule or separately validated fallback.

\paragraph{Exact context recovers quality, but not every shallow exit.}
We next reconstructed all 120 real bitstreams in the disjoint 24-image
validation cohort under the same locked route maps, replacing the deployed
zero-halo-plus-repair suffix with clipped sufficient-context windows and no
repair. Mean loss relative to full-frame e15 falls from 0.0889 to
0.0681\,dB; the paired improvement is 0.0208\,dB (24-image cluster-bootstrap
95\% interval 0.0155--0.0266\,dB). The count above 0.1\,dB falls from
35 to 24: 11 cases are rescued and no new threshold violation appears.
Two QP63 cases worsen slightly (at most 0.0035\,dB), consistent with the
old learned repair occasionally helping. The 24 persistent violations
are largely a depth-selection problem; e.g., image 0844/QP48 remains

The separate beta-fitting 24-image calibration partition shows a similar
0.0210\,dB mean recovery and 34-to-24 drop in violations; it is a mechanism
check, not an independent evaluation set.

For these maps the ideal union-of-required-cells model gives 20.55\%
mean synthesis MAC saving; 67/120 cases satisfy both exact-context loss
$\leq0.1$\,dB and ideal saving $\geq10$\%. These are \emph{potential}
operating points, not a latency result: the direct per-tile exact-window
code recomputes overlaps, while an efficient shared sparse implementation
has not been built. All comparisons here use e15 full-frame centred
YCbCr444, not released-decoder YUV611 or full-codec BD-rate.

persistent violations: uniformly raising all six tile exits by one group
recovers 22 cases below 0.1\,dB, and two more require a second group.

fallback would leave 17.89\% mean \emph{ideal} cohort MAC saving, versus
14.96\% from sending every violating case to all-deep. These numbers use
ground-truth image error to choose when to stop, so they are \emph{oracle
bounds}, not a trained router, a prospective held-out result, or measured
runtime. They motivate a calibrated risk signal and targeted capacity
increase rather than a blanket all-deep fallback.

A finer source-informed counterfactual advances only selected tiles by one
exit group. Eight of the 24 failures cross the cap after one tile, and a
further 14 after two to six tiles; 0844/QP32 and 0844/QP48 require the
uniform two-group increment. Choosing the fewest upgraded tiles and then
the highest ideal saving among ties yields 19.05\% mean ideal saving across
all 120 cases, versus 17.89\% for the uniform fallback. This 1.17-point
gap measures potential spatial targeting headroom under source feedback;
it does not establish a decoder-visible tile selector or sparse-kernel
latency. It also does not optimize arbitrary multi-group tile assignments.

We tested whether inexpensive latent statistics provide that signal for
the \emph{exact-context} failure label. A fixed L2 logistic model fitted on
calibration QP and map geometry reaches 0.755 AUC on the disjoint validation
images; adding stream length, latent moments, spatial variation, and
tile-aligned energy gives 0.760 AUC (paired image-bootstrap difference
95\% interval $[-0.088,0.080]$). At calibration-locked thresholds the
expanded model misses 11/24 validation violations while flagging 37/120
cases; the geometry-only model misses 9/24 while flagging 43/120.
The richer descriptor improves calibration fit but does not establish a
transferable guard. This validation set had already informed the mechanism
analysis, so the comparison is exploratory; a new prospective cohort is
needed before any risk-gated deployment claim.

\section{Auditing the Scalar Control Search}

For router scores $\ell_{t,k}$ and costs $c_k$, increasing the price $\beta$
in $k_t(\beta)=\arg\max_k(\ell_{t,k}-\beta c_k)$ cannot increase selected cost.
To see this, write the optimality inequalities at $\beta_1<\beta_2$ for
their respective choices $k_1,k_2$ and add them. The result is
\begin{equation}
 (\beta_2-\beta_1)(c_{k_1}-c_{k_2})\geq 0.
\end{equation}
No corresponding statement follows for source error $M_{t,k}$, because that
quantity is absent from the score being optimised. A feasibility test at the
most expensive endpoint can therefore miss a feasible middle assignment,
and distortion-based bisection can miss a later feasible interval.

We enumerate pairwise score-line intersections, representatives of the open
intervals between them, and the endpoints of the archived range
$[-20000,20000]$. Archived controls are included explicitly to retain their
floating-point tie behaviour. Identical maps are merged. Among feasible
maps, the selected candidate maximises modelled MAC saving, with table loss
as a tie-breaker. This is a finite-path audit in float64, rather than an
optimiser over all possible tile assignments.

The cost is monotone on every inspected router path, while source loss has
at least one reversal on 227/265 paths. At 0.05\,dB, enumeration improves
3 of 204 previously feasible plans, by 0.1626 percentage points on average,
and recovers one additional feasible case. At 0.1\,dB it improves 2 of 263
plans, by 0.0277 points on average. No paired saving changes at the four
larger targets. The largest missed saving is 30.45 points at 0.05\,dB, so a
small mean difference should not be read as a uniform per-frame bound.

We strengthen the blind baseline in the same way. For ordered dithering,
all changes occur at a finite set of Bayer-rank thresholds and rung
boundaries. Enumerating those maps reveals source-loss reversals on 232/265
paths. It improves 3/204 paired plans at 0.05\,dB and 1/263 at 0.1\,dB,
with mean gains of 0.0192 and 0.0028 points, respectively. Six additional
cases become feasible at 0.05\,dB. The quality of these newly selected maps
has not been measured after final mixed reconstruction, so they do not
replace the main manuscript's reconstruction curves.

The audit reproduces every archived feasible control and cost, verifies
cost monotonicity, and checks that enumeration never discards a better
archived feasible saving. An independent three-exit example verifies the
non-monotone failure mechanism against a dense control grid.

\section{Sequence-Disjoint Control Calibration}
\label{sec:crossfit}

The source-calibrated sweep answers which allocation a policy can attain
when its scalar control is tuned for each image. It does not establish what
happens when that control is fixed before seeing a new source image.
We therefore add a CPU diagnostic that keeps the router checkpoint and
archived tile tables fixed while separating control calibration by sequence.
All five QPs of a sequence stay in one fold. Sorting sequence-name hashes
and assigning ranks modulo five yields folds of 10/10/11/11/11 sequences.
For each QP, controls are fitted on four folds and evaluated on the fifth;
each of the 265 frame--QP pairs is evaluated exactly once per setting.
The data have previously informed development, so this is a calibration
diagnostic, not an untouched external test.

\paragraph{Sequence identity does not establish content independence.}
We subsequently screen all 1,378 first-frame pairs without using quality
losses or router outputs. A fixed criterion combines a 63-bit low-frequency
DCT fingerprint distance of at most six with centred $64\times64$ luma
correlation of at least 0.98. Two pairs are flagged across folds:
the 832- and 416-pixel-wide RaceHorses variants (distance 0, correlation
0.99267), and \texttt{videoSRC17} with \texttt{Kimono1} (2, 0.98039).
Different raw hashes do not exclude related visual content. These are
screening candidates, not proof of exhaustive source or temporal identity.
Conservatively joining them produces 51 proposed content groups for future
splits. We do not change the existing folds or reselect their results after
this audit; their scope remains sequence-disjoint development evidence,
not content-independent generalisation.

We search a fixed router grid of 4,097 prices in $[-256,256]$, which reaches
both all-shallow and all-deep endpoints on every frame. Dither uses 385
mixture controls from zero to three; uniform uses the four reachable depths.
Among controls satisfying a training-fold mean or 90th-percentile table-loss
target, we maximise mean MAC saving. If none satisfies the target, we use
the lowest training-risk control and explicitly retain its infeasibility.
No test-source-conditioned fallback is applied. A held-out-label
perturbation check confirms that test losses cannot change the fitted control.

At a 0.1\,dB mean target, router and dither save 27.379\% and 24.608\%
MACs, with mean held-out losses of 0.1006 and 0.0997\,dB. Nevertheless,
122/265 and 123/265 cases exceed the target. Fitting the training-fold
90th percentile reduces these counts to 32 and 29, while savings become
17.586\% and 15.558\%. At the tighter 0.05\,dB Q90 target, calibration
groups covering 159/265 test cases are infeasible for each policy; these
cases remain in every reported aggregate.

\section{Separating the Histogram from Spatial Placement}

A map can save arithmetic by selecting many cheap exits even if their
locations contain no useful information. To isolate placement, let
$p_k=N^{-1}\sum_t\mathbf{1}[k_t=k]$ be its fixed exit histogram and let
$\pi$ be a uniformly random permutation of its assignments. Each tile has
marginal exit probability $p_k$, giving the exact expectation
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[\frac1N\sum_t M_{t,k_{\pi(t)}}\right]
 =\frac1N\sum_t\sum_k p_k M_{t,k}.
 \label{eq:permutation}
\end{equation}
We report $G=10\log_{10}(\mathbb E_\pi[\overline M_\pi]/\overline M)$,
where $\overline M$ is the recorded map's table MSE. Positive $G$ indicates
useful spatial placement at exactly the same histogram and MAC cost.
The logarithm is outside the expectation: this is not expected PSNR over
random reconstructions.

At 0.1\,dB, source-informed search has a mean placement gain of 0.0321\,dB,
the router 0.0213\,dB, and dithering 0.0007\,dB over their respective shuffled
histograms. The router's 95\% sequence-cluster interval is
[0.0167,0.0262]\,dB; dithering's is [$-0.0002$,0.0017]\,dB. Uniform-depth
maps have zero gain by construction. Among the 263 paired router maps,
204 have positive gain, seven have negative gain and 52 use one exit only.
This diagnoses content association conditional on a source-calibrated
histogram; it does not validate held-out autonomous calibration.

For each map we also solve the exact fixed-histogram assignment problem on
$M$. Repeating each exit column according to its count produces a square
linear assignment problem. Its minimum table MSE gives a conditional upper
bound on placement value. The router's mean regret to that optimum is
0.0044\,dB at the 0.1\,dB target. Every source-search map attains its own
fixed-histogram optimum, as expected for a separable Lagrangian minimiser.
The optimum still requires the complete source table and carries the same
information-acquisition cost. These exact table expectations are distinct
from the following finite-permutation reconstruction intervention.

\paragraph{Actual reconstruction at unchanged depth proportions.}
We replay all 53 QP32 frames using the fixed Q90 router and dither maps.
Each original map is paired with three predetermined permutations,
using seeds 202609280--202609282 and identical permutation indices for
both policies on a frame. Assignments are shuffled only within strata of
equal valid tile height and width. This preserves both padded depth counts
and the valid-pixel area assigned to each depth. All 424 map cases are
retained, including unchanged maps; caching identical maps requires 373
distinct reconstructions. Original-map MSE reproduces the earlier CPU replay.

For sequence $i$ and policy $p$, we compute
\begin{equation}
 G_{i,p}=10\log_{10}\!\left[
 \frac{\frac13\sum_{s=1}^{3}D^{\rm RGB}_{i,p,s}}
 {D^{\rm RGB}_{i,p,\mathrm{original}}}\right],
\end{equation}
then average sequences equally. Router and dither gains are 0.01393\,dB
[0.00949,0.01930] and 0.00036\,dB [$-0.00031$,0.00105], respectively.
Their paired difference is 0.01357\,dB [0.00920,0.01897]; resampling the
51 candidate-content groups gives [0.00923,0.01883]. The intervals use
5,000 paired draws and condition on the three sampled permutations.
They exclude permutation Monte Carlo uncertainty and training variation.
This intervention measures placement within each policy's own depth
distribution. It neither matches the two policies' achieved RD nor repairs
the development-used calibration split. Table-optimal-map replay remains
unevaluated; no bitstream or latency claim follows from these neural outputs.

\section{Shared-Exit Training Provenance}

The released warm start, shared-exit fine-tuning and independent depth
trainings are three distinct checkpoints/protocols. The archived shared-exit
metadata lists 379,614 training images, batch size eight and 16 configured
fine-tuning epochs. The inspected launcher selects 512-pixel crops through
the late part of the upstream schedule, freezes the encoder/entropy path,
and enables mixed-tile training, depth-scaled adapters, grid repair,
adjacent feature distillation and a full-depth anchor. Its anchor and
distillation coefficients are 10 and 1, respectively; the anchor code scales
its batch-mean reconstruction error by the batch-mean $\lambda$ unless the
separate per-sample option is enabled. The e15 checkpoint stores weights,
epoch and architectural configuration, not the complete launch command.
Thus the launcher and metadata document the inspected intended setup,
rather than proving every historical process argument.

The evaluated router checkpoint explicitly stores live inputs
\texttt{stem,qp}, soft-label cross-entropy, temperature ratio 0.1,
training cost weight $1.3\times10^{-5}$, 48 stem projection channels and
256 hidden units. It has no spatial-context convolution. General-purpose
latent/scale/bit pathways remain in the class, but do not provide variable
content to this checkpoint. The decoder feature is pooled by tile mean and
standard deviation, and the QP is divided by 63. Checkpoint inspection is
recorded in \texttt{design\_audit.json}. This corrects the broader description
one would obtain by reading the router class defaults alone.

For tile errors $M_{t,k}$, exit costs $c_k$ and
$L_{t,k}=M_{t,k}+\lambda_c c_k$, the softened target is
\begin{equation}
 p^*_{t,k}\propto\exp\!\left[-\frac{L_{t,k}-\min_j L_{t,j}}
 {\tau\,\max(\max_j L_{t,j}-\min_j L_{t,j},\epsilon)}\right].
 \label{eq:softtarget}
\end{equation}
The range normalisation controls the sharpness separately for each tile;
$\lambda_c=1.3\times10^{-5}$ and $\tau=0.1$. This target is combined with
the recorded regret term. It supervises a relative exit preference and
does not calibrate the assembled image's loss.

The companion run record matches the checkpoint path, codec epoch, live
inputs and stored evaluation statistics. It records 6,000 updates, batch
size six, 512-pixel crops, seed zero, initial learning rate $10^{-3}$ and a
regret coefficient of one. The inspected trainer uses AdamW with weight
decay $10^{-4}$, cosine scheduling and gradient clipping at norm one on
Open Images. Its online agreement monitor withholds tiles within training
images; the final label-agreement pass draws fresh images from the same
shuffled epoch. Neither statistic is used as the manuscript's reconstruction
or held-out calibration result. The stored head contains 148,176 parameters,
including its inactive input branches.

\section{The Independent Depth Study}

The ongoing experiment retains the first two, four or six synthesis trunk
blocks of the DCVC-UF intra architecture and trains each complete codec
under the pinned official recipe. The encoder and entropy weights learn
alongside the decoder. Each model consequently has its own representation,
distinct from the shared-latent exits evaluated in the main paper.
The six-expert selector remains a proposed deployment path, not an
implemented result.

Architectural parameter counts for all six depths are plotted in
\cref{fig:scenario}a. D2/D4/D6 have completed all 105 epochs in the

\paragraph{The pinned training protocol.}
The source is Microsoft's image-training recipe at commit
\texttt{cbdae87a5445}~\cite{microsoft2026training}. All 384,795 images in
Open Images train\_0/1/2 are retained as lossless decoded-RGB PNGs, with
pixel equality checked during preparation. Each independent model uses
seed 42, Xavier initialisation, batch size 16, AdamW with the upstream
defaults, and gradient clipping at norm 0.1. QP is sampled from 0--63 with

105-epoch schedule. There are 24,049 batches per epoch and 2,525,145 scheduled updates

Training uses FP32 with the upstream TF32 flags and

PyTorch random-number path. AMP is disabled. The instrumented eager step
was checked against the upstream step at identical inputs and RNG state.

\paragraph{Isolating depth.}
Released D12 is a useful deployment anchor, but a D12 trained with the same
data manifest, 105-epoch schedule, optimisation and initialisation protocol
is necessary to isolate depth from training history. D8 and D10 should be
prioritised after the initial depth frontier and this control are available.
The main runs retain the official recipe; distillation and prefix
initialisation are separate variants rather than silent training changes.

\paragraph{Isolating routing.}
The evaluation should progress from full-frame D12 to patchwise D12, the
best validation-selected fixed expert, blind mixtures, simple image
statistics, the MLP and source-informed search. Full-frame versus patchwise
D12 measures the cost of tiling and entropy resets before routing receives
credit for any gain. A histogram-preserving shuffle separates the chosen
expert proportions from their spatial placement.
A model below the dataset-mean frontier may still help a subset of patches.
Expert removal should therefore be evaluated by leave-one-expert-out
routing at matched constraints, not justified by mean full-image quality
alone.

\paragraph{A concrete router hypothesis.}
A lightweight encoder-side predictor can estimate each expert's distortion
and rate relative to a cheap reference. Let $z_{t,k}$ indicate the expert
assigned to patch $t$ and $n_k=\sum_t z_{t,k}$. For equal-shaped patches and
measured grouped-runtime tables $\tau_k(n_k)$, a proposed system objective is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat J=\sum_{t,k}z_{t,k}(\widehat D_{t,k}+\lambda\widehat R_{t,k})
 +\mu\sum_k\tau_k(n_k).
 \label{eq:proposed}
\end{equation}
Routing, packing and scatter/gather work must also enter the measured total;
the expression isolates the expert-runtime dependence. An independent
per-patch latency label approximates this coupled problem. A bounded
post-processing variant could move a patch from $k$ to $j$ according to its
predicted RD change and the measured marginal time
$\tau_k(n_k-1)-\tau_k(n_k)+\tau_j(n_j+1)-\tau_j(n_j)$.
The number of passes and decision-time limit should be fixed on validation
images. Any improvement must include this extra control work.

Patch counts alone cease to describe the workload when neighbouring regions
are coalesced or frame edges create smaller windows. Then the assignment
induces a set of coded regions, each with its own shape and context. Runtime
tables must condition on expert, shape, QP and the validated execution mode;
packing regions of different shapes into a hypothetical common batch is not
an implemented optimisation. Region construction also changes repeated
context, entropy resets and headers. Accordingly, the fixed-map merge control
separates these execution choices from prediction accuracy. A same-histogram
spatial shuffle is a useful additional control: equal expert frequencies need
not imply equal grouping opportunities or equal codec cost.

Regression or regret-weighted training should be compared against ordinary
exit-label cross-entropy. A second-stage top-two verification can be
restricted to small predicted margins, with its threshold selected on
held-out calibration images and its extra encoding work included. Neither
a large prediction margin nor a low average regression error establishes a
framewise quality guarantee. These are prospective experiments, not measured
components of the current shared-exit system.

Spatial competition between complete codecs already provides a close
precedent~\cite{blard2026spatial}; adaptive compression and patch early exit
provide further controls~\cite{tao2023adanic,hu2023cgslim,wang2022ape}.
The testable direction here is depth-heterogeneous selection with measured
rate--distortion--time costs and reduced encoder search. Practical codec
studies motivate including device execution and perceptual
assessment~\cite{jia2025dcvcrt,tatwawadi2026practical}.

\section{Evaluation Contract for the Next Stage}

Expert training, router training, calibration and final evaluation should be
split at image level. Patches from the same image must stay in one split.
The four fixed validation crops in the current training monitor are health
checks; a broader intermediate evaluation should use the available 100
DIV2K validation images, followed by the declared final Kodak/CTC/CLIC
scope. The frozen epoch-20 DIV2K crop evaluation below is complete;
the final trained-model, full-image and native-runtime study remains pending.

Each codec needs a true-byte rate--distortion curve. Equal QP does not imply
equal rate across independently learned codecs. Comparisons should use their
common measured support, reporting infeasible targets and avoiding
extrapolation. Rate includes main and hyperprior payloads, model identity,
quality/control parameters and framing headers, divided by original pixels.
Quality is measured after cropping valid pixels; the exact RGB/YUV formula
and perceptual metrics are declared before test-set selection.

Timing requires the same GPU, precision, kernel path and warmup, with
alternated paired execution order and retained raw trials. Encoder,
decoder and complete round-trip times are separate outcomes. CUDA-event
module time and end-to-end wall time must remain separately labelled.
Grouping, scatter/gather, entropy work, stream parsing, transfers and fallback
are included where they occur. The full codec bank's resident weight memory
and peak activation memory are both reported.

The primary success criterion is a positive net runtime advantage over the
best validation-selected blind control on common achieved rate--distortion
support. Critical comparisons should then be repeated across training
seeds. A content bootstrap at fixed weights measures a different source of
variation and cannot replace those repeats.

\section{Analytical Scenarios for the Pending Runtime Measurements}

The independent depth study has an interim CPU RD diagnostic, but no
final trained-model RD or measured native-latency frontier yet.
To make the design hypothesis inspectable, \cref{fig:scenario} separates
exact architectural parameter counts from a hypothetical runtime model.
For retained depth $d$, let $f$ be the assumed fraction of full-depth time
spent in the twelve-block trunk and let $h$ be added routing/grouping overhead
normalised by the original full-depth time. Under linear trunk-time scaling,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{T(d)}{T(12)}=(1-f)+f\frac{d}{12}+h.
 \label{eq:scenario}
\end{equation}
We sweep $f\in\{0.5,0.7,0.9\}$ and $h\in\{0,0.03\}$. These are explicit
sensitivity assumptions, not fractions estimated from the hardware.
The reciprocal of \cref{eq:scenario} is the plotted scenario speedup.
It shows why removing most trunk blocks need not yield a proportional
reduction in complete codec time. The curves deliberately contain no
predicted PSNR, bitrate or empirical uncertainty bands.

Exact decoder size is $1,767,168+1,038,720d$ parameters; adding the
27,947,520 non-synthesis parameters gives complete-codec size. This formula
matches all three independent training manifests. It describes capacity,
not compute: retained tensors, arithmetic and measured device time are
separate quantities. The data file labels these scenarios explicitly and
records every assumption used to generate them.
A separate CPU construction of all six depths verifies these counts and
exact equality of their retained initial tensors to the seed-matched D12
initialisation; it performs no training or quality evaluation.
